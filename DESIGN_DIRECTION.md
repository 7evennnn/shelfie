# Shelfie design direction

## Quality bar

Shelfie must never look or sound like a generic AI-generated website. A clean, working interface is only the baseline. Every public-facing choice needs a clear reason, product context and point of view.

The intended character is friendly, informal, slightly whimsical and easy to share with random people. It is not corporate, luxurious, motivational or written like store copy.

## Voice

Write the way a normal person would talk about their books.

- Prefer direct labels: **Books**, **Recaps**, **Add a book**, **Save recap**
- State useful facts or actions, not positioning claims
- Keep headings short and concrete
- Use full stops sparingly in short interface copy; they can feel unnecessarily stern
- A small smiley is welcome where it feels natural, but should not become a gimmick
- Read every line aloud before keeping it

Delete a line if it only reassures people about a basic expectation. Shelfie does not need to announce that it has no quiz, no opaque algorithm, no personality test or no account unless that information answers a real question at that moment.

### Copy patterns to avoid

- Split slogans with arbitrary coloured emphasis
- “X turns Y into Z — ready to [three verbs]”
- “No X. No Y. Just Z.”
- Vague two-part slogans such as “Build it once. Keep it here.”
- Pseudo-editorial labels such as “reader identity,” “living edition,” “periodic editions” or “the shape of it”
- Abstract nouns that could belong to any lifestyle product
- Repeating the same explanation in a heading, subheading, card and footer
- Statements such as “updated automatically” or “made from the books you logged” when the interface already makes that obvious

A useful test: **Would a real Shelfie user say this to a friend?** If not, rewrite or remove it.

## Visual direction

- Keep the approved Shelfie header typography
- Use a more whimsical display face inside shareable cards and recaps
- Favour the original warm card palette and editorial structure
- Keep the red book-count square with its offset shadow
- Every large number must say what it counts
- Do not add giant letters, monograms, blobs or background glyphs without a clear purpose
- Avoid gradients, glass effects, card stacks, pill-heavy navigation and generic component-library composition
- Decorative choices should come from books, shelves, print, marginalia or library ephemera
- Friendly does not mean childish; polished does not mean corporate

## Structure

The reading card is the primary product. Books and monthly/yearly recaps are supporting tools.

- Open on the card
- Let the card do the explaining
- Keep book management practical and quiet
- Make recaps feel like designed keepsakes, not dashboards
- Do not add marketing sections beneath working product screens

## Review checklist

Before showing or shipping a design:

1. Remove copy that states a baseline expectation
2. Remove any sentence that could be pasted into an unrelated app
3. Check that decorative elements have a product-specific reason
4. Give every prominent number and control immediate context
5. Check for overlaps and odd symbols at desktop and mobile sizes
6. Test the actual interactions, not only the source
7. Read the complete page aloud for tone
8. Compare against the quality bar: this must not look like a ten-second AI build
